why would you do this

honestly i had the idea for like 5 years. it started off as a joke, then i made a few cardboard mockups, then it became "hehe how funny would it be if the final act of my university education was a nya neko catboy tail. also i couldn't come up with something better. i miraculously found a professor willing to be my supervisor.

the idea is pretty basic though. a set of sensors read your vitals. specifically autonomic nervous system vitals like blood volume pulse (BVP), and electrodermal activity (EDA, also reffered to as galvanic skin response, GSR). bvp is a measure of how much blood in your veins at any given moment. if you take lots and lots of measurements every second you get a graph that looks like this:

looks like a cardiogram doesn't it? cuz it is. the peaks represent a heart beat. from that you can get a metric called heart rate variability (HRV), which tends to fluctuate with stress. combine that with EDA which happens to be one of the best physical responses to mental stress. you can estimate a person's stress level/emotional state.

then using some fancy algorithms you map a floating point stress value to tail movements using some sinusoid equation (based on some research about human perception of cat tail meanings) and boom. tail that wags quicker when you get excited.

the tail

the overwhelming consensus is it looks like a robot dildo. if by chance you work at lovense or bad dragon, hmu.

the reason it looks like a zased and xhristpilled femboy twitch streamer's funkopop is because it's modeled after a spine, the rubber bands act as tendons that oppose gravity and keep the segments (or vertebrae) perpendicular to the user's body. this lowers how much work the servos need to do significantly, all they gotta do is oppose the rubber bands.

i would love to add some insane mathematical equations here showing how the mechanics and physics of this works, truth is i didn't calculate shit. this was all done by trying different rubber bands and servos until i found a good combo.

servos

these loud whiny moany little guys move the tail by pulling a string that runs through each vertebrae segment, eventually being attached at the tip.

you'd think we're working in 3d here, but the nature of [i forgot the robotics term for it] kinematics means it's just two dimensions, as the tail will curve inwards towards the user as it's pulled to one direction. the code only really has to translate x-y coordinates to degrees for each servo.
